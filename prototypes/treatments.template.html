Six ways to put a clock on a watercolor

These paintings were made to be looked at on a table, not behind a clock, a date, three notifications and twelve app labels. Most of every sheet is bare cream rag paper, so white text disappears — and where the paint is dense, dark text disappears instead.

Same painting in all six phones, same clock, same notifications. The only thing that changes is how the picture and the interface negotiate — from not touching the image at all, to reframing it completely.

Every plate is reduced to an 8 × 16 grid of local contrast. Busy cells are where a thin clock digit gets lost in brushwork; flat cells are safe to write on, whatever their colour. The green band marks the calmest strip quiet-crop found, and it is the strip that treatment slides under the clock.

flat — safe textured busy — avoid

Changing it without you noticing

An hourly wallpaper that swaps while you are reading the screen is a distraction. The fix is not a slower timer — it is to only ever change across a dark boundary, so every change happens in a moment you were not looking.

On screen off

Swap in the dark, never on a tick

The change is triggered by the screen going off, not by the clock reaching the hour. On Android that is Engine.onVisibilityChanged(false); the ACTION_SCREEN_OFF broadcast is the fallback. The timer only decides whether a swap is due.

≥ 60 min since last

One painting per hour, not per glance

Gate on now − lastChange ≥ 1h. Checking the time fifteen times in a minute should not burn fifteen paintings — you would never see any of them.

Before wake

Decode while it is still dark

Crop, scale and compose the next plate while the screen is off, and hold the finished bitmap. Waking should reveal a picture that is already there — no fade, no load, no visible moment of change.

Always-on display

Screen off is not the same as unwatched

With an always-on display the screen is technically off and still visible. Suppress the swap while AOD is showing and let it happen at the next real blackout.

Order

Shuffle a ring, don't roll dice

Walk a shuffled ring of all 7,581 plates rather than picking at random, so nothing repeats until the whole collection has been seen. At one an hour that is 316 days before you meet the same painting twice.

Exception

Hold a plate you reached for

If you tapped through to read what a painting is, don't take it away an hour later. Anything you interacted with pins until you dismiss it.

Platform reality. Android gives you all of this through a WallpaperService, lock screen included. iOS has no third-party live wallpapers — the closest equivalents are the built-in Photos shuffle (which already changes on lock, but won't apply any of these treatments) or a Shortcuts automation on Sleep Focus that swaps a pre-rendered image. Which phone is this for? It decides whether the treatments below can be computed on device per painting, or have to be baked into a folder of finished images ahead of time.

What I'd want to know

  1. Which treatment survives a week? Full-bleed drama photographs well and grates by Thursday; the quieter ones tend to win on a device you unlock ninety times a day.
  2. Is the handwriting an asset or a mess? Every sheet carries the artist's pencil notes — variety, date, the grower's county. botanical-plate treats them as the point; the full-bleed treatments crop them off entirely.
  3. Does the clock have to stay put? quiet-crop is the only one that moves the painting to suit the clock. It reads best when the paintings differ a lot, and it is also the only one that can surprise you.
