Three and a half plates wide

The phone problem was that the screen is narrower than the paintings — 0.46 against 0.65 — so filling it crops the width. The monitor is the opposite problem, and a much bigger one. At 3440 × 1440 the screen is 2.39:1: three and a half plates wide. Filling it with one painting would throw away about 85% of it.

So none of these fill the screen with one plate. Each is a different answer to the question the ultrawide actually asks — what goes in all that width — and they are ordered by how far they drift from "a painting on a wall".

Changing it without you noticing

The phone prototype settled this already: a wallpaper that swaps while you are looking at the screen is a distraction, and the fix is not a slower timer but a dark boundary. The desktop has the same boundary and announces it on D-Bus.

On screen dark

Swap behind the lock, never on a tick

xfce4-screensaver broadcasts ActiveChanged on org.xfce.ScreenSaver. The watcher composes the next plate when that goes true, so unlocking reveals a picture that was already there.

≥ 1 hour since last

One painting per hour, not per coffee

Locking the screen six times before lunch should not burn six paintings — you would never actually see any of them. The gate is time since the last change, not events.

Order

Shuffle a ring, don't roll dice

Selection walks one shuffled order of the pool and remembers its place, so nothing repeats until everything has been seen. Preparing more scans shuffles them into the part not yet shown rather than restarting.
